Privacy policy

Lettrine asks nothing of you, and we keep nothing about you. Everything the game remembers stays on your phone.

And the game sends none of your game data. Your grids, your words, your garden and your settings never leave your phone. Unlike many games, the ad service does not start at launch: it starts only when you touch the line offering a hint in exchange for an ad. If you never touch it, it collects nothing.

One reservation, which we would rather state than hide: Lettrine is displayed through WebView, an Android component supplied and updated by Google. That component may contact Google on its own account, for instance to configure itself. The game hands it no data to transmit, and we have no control over it.

Who publishes Lettrine

Lettrine is developed by Fial Labs. For any question about this policy, or to exercise your rights: fiallabs@gmail.com

What Lettrine collects

Fial Labs collects nothing. The game does not ask for an account, a name, an email address or a phone number. It does not request your location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera. We have installed no analytics tool, no crash reporter and no tracking code of our own.

WebView, the Android component that displays the game, is supplied by Google and may exchange data with Google for its own operation; what it transmits falls under Google's policy, not ours; the game hands it no data to transmit.

Google's ad service does collect data — but only if you give it the occasion. It does not initialise when the game launches. It initialises when you touch the offer to get a hint by watching an ad, and not before. From that moment, and each time an ad plays, it collects, under the names Google Play uses:

  • Device or other identifiers — your device's advertising identifier and its App Set ID;
  • Approximate location — derived from your IP address, never from GPS. The game never asks for Android's location permission and never reads your position; Google infers a region from the network address that every connection carries;
  • App interactions — the ad request and the playback of ad videos;
  • Diagnostics — loading time, freezes, energy use.

These go to Google and to nobody else, for advertising, measurement, fraud prevention and security. They travel encrypted in transit (TLS). Google keeps them under its own policy; we never see them and receive no copy.

What Lettrine stores on your device

Everything is stored locally, in the app's private storage, and is never transmitted anywhere:

  • your level — Tranquille, Normal or Corsé;
  • the grid in progress — its level, its letters, its seed, its day, the words placed, the letters revealed, the hints used and the order of your rack;
  • the games you left when switching level, so you find them exactly as they were on returning;
  • your stock of hints — three at first launch, five at most;
  • the time from which the next time-given hint is counted — one comes back every eight hours, up to the ceiling;
  • per level, the number of the last day whose grid you finished, and how many free grids you have opened;
  • your garden — the number of days on which you finished at least one grid, and the number of the last of those days. This is what makes the vine grow in the margin;
  • where you were — the open page, the chosen word and the letters typed — to bring you back there if Android closed the app while you were elsewhere;
  • your settings — gesture sounds (off by default), ambience (off by default) and vibration (on by default), plus the fact that the sound offer has already been shown to you once.

All of this disappears entirely if you uninstall the app or clear its data from Android's settings. We hold no copy of it, and we cannot restore it.

The recovery code

Lettrine gives you a four-word recovery code, to carry your garden to another phone if you change device.

We would rather say precisely what it is: it is computed on your device, it carries your garden only — the number of finished days and the number of the last one — and it is sent nowhere. There is no server to send it to. You alone know it, you alone hold it, and if you lose it we cannot give it back, because we never had it. It carries neither your hints, nor your finished grids, nor your games in progress.

Hints

The game checks that a grid can still be finished, and offers hints. All of that is computed on your phone. No grid, no word and no position is ever sent to a server: there is no server.

Advertising

Lettrine never forces an ad on you. No ad appears at launch, between grids, or at any moment you did not choose. There are no interstitials at all. It is a studio rule, and two automated checks enforce it when the app is built.

The game offers a rewarded ad in one place only, and always at your request: when your stock of hints is empty, and at most twice per grid. The reward is one hint. The offer only appears on screen if an ad is actually loaded; otherwise it does not show, rather than promising you something that is not available. The stock also refills on its own, through play and with time, with no ad at all.

These ads are served by Google AdMob. We would rather say it exactly: the ad service does not initialise when the game launches. Consent and ad loading are born of your gesture, at the moment you touch the offer. On that occasion, and each time an ad plays, AdMob collects the four types of data listed above, along with technical details such as device model and Android version, to select and measure the ads shown. Everything is sent over an encrypted connection (TLS) and processed by Google under its own privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy. We have no access to it and receive no copy of it.

Your consent and your ad preferences

If you live in the European Union, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, a consent form is shown to you before any ad, at the moment you first ask for one. It carries a “Do not consent” button as visible as the accept button.

On any Android device, you can also reset or delete your advertising identifier, or opt out of personalized advertising, from your device's settings (Settings › Google › Ads).

Android permissions

The game declares two permissions on its own behalf:

  • INTERNET — for the ad service: loading an ad when you ask for one, and showing it. The game's code uses it for nothing else. WebView, the Android component that displays the game, also uses it on its own account (see above);
  • VIBRATE — for the very short vibration when a letter settles, and the double one when a word is accepted. A switch turns it off, on the game’s “Paramètres” (Settings) page, opened from the menu.

The AdMob library brings its own, which we prefer to name rather than hide: ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (whether the network is available), AD_ID and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_* (the advertising identifier and Android's advertising services), WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE (used by Google while an ad plays). None of these permissions is classed as “dangerous” by Android: they give access neither to your files, nor to your contacts, nor to your device's position. The approximate location Google records comes from your IP address, not from any of these permissions.

What can be bought in the game

Nothing. There is no real-money purchase, no payment, and no banking data. The stock of hints refills through play and with time, or through an ad if you choose. The garden's vine grows by playing, never through an ad or a payment.

Children

Lettrine is intended for a general audience and is not designed for children under 13. The app is not enrolled in Google Play's “Designed for Families” program. We collect no data ourselves, about children or anyone else. What Google's ad service collects, listed above, applies to every player alike; that is one more reason the game is reserved for people aged 13 or over.

Your rights

The European General Data Protection Regulation gives you rights of access, rectification, erasure and objection over personal data concerning you.

We hold no personal data about you, so there is nothing to consult, correct or erase on our side. The game's information is on your device, under your sole control. For data processed by Google for advertising, requests go directly to Google, through myaccount.google.com. The step-by-step procedure is in Delete your data, below.

You may still write to us at fiallabs@gmail.com with any question, and you have the right to lodge a complaint with your data protection authority (in France, the CNIL).

Transfers outside the European Union

Our own code transmits nothing. The data Google AdMob collects, if you ask for an ad, may be processed outside the European Union, under the conditions described in Google's privacy policy.

Delete your data

This is the section Google Play links to under “delete data”. It names every piece of data that exists, where it lives, how to delete it, and what remains afterwards.

1. What is on your device — your settings, your stock of hints, your games, your garden. To delete all of it: uninstall the app, or open Android's Settings › Apps › Lettrine › Storage and choose “Clear storage”. Everything is deleted at once, and nothing of it is kept anywhere: there is no server and no copy at Fial Labs. We cannot restore it. If you want to keep your garden, write down your recovery code first.

2. What Google's ad service holds, if you have asked for at least one ad — the four types listed above. We hold none of it and cannot delete it for you; Google can. On your device, Settings › Google › Ads lets you delete your advertising identifier, after which apps no longer receive it, or reset it, after which what was collected before is no longer tied to the new one. For the data Google keeps under its own policy, use Google's own tools at myaccount.google.com, or write to Google as described in its privacy policy.

3. What Fial Labs keeps — nothing. There is no account to close and no record to erase on our side, so there is nothing you need to ask us to delete. If you write to fiallabs@gmail.com to ask, we will confirm it in writing.
